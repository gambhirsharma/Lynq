01 The problem

The bent-pipe limit

Most satellite IoT today uses bent-pipe satellites. They only relay signals, so a ground gateway has to be in view. When that gateway is out of view, blocked, overloaded or restricted by national rules, the service drops.

Offshore wind farms, oil and gas platforms, vessels and pipelines are hit hardest. Their traffic is mixed: alarms and control commands need priority, while telemetry, video and files can wait.

A regenerative satellite runs a full 5G base station (gNB) in orbit, so traffic can be routed over inter-satellite links or held onboard. That also makes the network much harder to run: Doppler, handovers, intermittent feeder links, gateway selection and limited onboard compute. Lynq is the software layer that handles it.

02 The solution

Lynq NTN-Pulse

Digital twin

One model of satellites, coverage, gateway visibility, feeder and ISL state, terminals, traffic demand and onboard compute. Sized for today's ~12 satellites and the planned 30.

Policy engine

Assigns each traffic class to a path (deliver now, relay, process at the edge, or store and forward) based on QoS, latency, reliability, data residency and cost.

Sovereign routing

The onboard gNB knows each user's identity, network and verified location, so every flow goes only to a gateway that is allowed to receive it, with local breakout.

NTN dashboard

Timing, beam management, handovers, link budgets, scheduling, queues, predicted outages and every routing decision, in one live view.

Three traffic classes, sorted in orbit

▲ Critical

Alarms and safety or shutdown commands. A few hundred bytes. Takes the first uplink slot and the first authorised path on the current pass.

● Regular

Telemetry, logs and files. Queued onboard and stored and forwarded on a later pass, within 90 minutes.

◆ Metadata

Device status, position, delivery receipts and policy tags. Tiny, but it tells the twin where each asset is and which gateway it may use.

03 The proposal

Technical, regulatory and business

Our two-page answer to OQ's three expected outcomes: a defined constellation, a regulatory pathway and a first-order business case.

Constellation

We start with today's ~12 satellites (OQ's 10 plus 2 ERMIS) and size the twin for the planned 30, focused on the North Sea offshore energy basin (~51–61°N).

At ~500–550 km and 10° minimum elevation, one footprint is ~3,000 km across, so a single satellite in view covers the whole basin and every land gateway around it. Scaling to 30 (e.g. 3 planes × 10) shrinks the gaps between passes about 2.5× and lets each plane close an RF inter-satellite ring.

Payload

Full regenerative gNB on every satellite. Bent-pipe cannot serve users with no gateway in view, and a split gNB still round-trips control signalling over the feeder link. Power and radiation costs are handled with a narrowband gNB, duty-cycling over empty ocean and COTS processing under a rad-hard supervisor.

The Consultant's Dilemma

A local gateway may be the best technical choice but still be off-limits because of national restrictions, data-residency rules or licensing. Lynq applies routing policy onboard and sends each flow only to a policy-compliant gateway. Under MNO partnerships, the MNO keeps lawful intercept.

ITU Radio Regulations Article 9 coordination and Article 22 EPFD limits are hard design constraints. We support EU-hosted processing, encryption, customer key ownership, pseudonymisation and GDPR retention. Sovereignty is built into routing.

First buyer

A European offshore energy operator or offshore wind O&M provider. Lynq reduces blind spots, improves alarm delivery and cuts dependence on VSAT and fixed gateways.

Revenue

Integration fees, monthly subscriptions by asset and traffic class, usage charges for priority QoS, and MNO wholesale or roaming. We price per asset, never per byte.
