01 The problem

The bent-pipe limit

Most satellite IoT today uses bent-pipe satellites. They only relay signals, so a ground gateway has to be in view. When that gateway is out of view, blocked, overloaded or restricted by national rules, the service drops.

Offshore wind farms, oil and gas platforms, vessels and pipelines are hit hardest. Their traffic is mixed: alarms and control commands need priority, while telemetry, video and files can wait.

A regenerative satellite runs a full 5G base station (gNB) in orbit, so traffic can be routed over inter-satellite links or held onboard. That also makes the network much harder to run: a gNB on every satellite, dozens of gateways, hundreds of thousands of devices, and links that come and go every few minutes. Updating software or AI models by hand does not scale. Lynq designs the network and the software that runs it.

02 The solution

Lynq NTN-Pulse

7×16 polar constellation

112 satellites at 700 km with Ka-band RF crosslinks. 100% of Earth every minute, alarm p99 98 ms. Chosen by searching 3,064 designs on the MeluXina supercomputer.

Policy engine + digital twin

Picks the path for every message: deliver now, relay over crosslinks, or store and forward. Lands data only at gateways allowed to receive it, with local breakout.

KubeEdge orchestration

One control plane from cloud to gateways, satellites and devices. Updates roll out canary-first, plane by plane, and nodes keep serving when the link drops.

AI-enabled IoT devices

Models run on the terminal and on the satellite, so the narrowband link carries alarms and summaries, not raw data.

Three traffic classes, sorted in orbit

▲ Critical

Alarms and safety or shutdown commands. A few hundred bytes. Takes the first uplink slot and the first authorised path on the current pass.

● Regular

Telemetry, logs and files. Queued onboard and stored and forwarded on a later pass, within 90 minutes.

◆ Metadata

Device status, position, delivery receipts and policy tags. Tiny, but it tells the twin where each asset is and which gateway it may use.

03 The proposal

Technical, regulatory and business

Our answer to OQ's three expected outcomes, as pitched at NEOSPACE Europe 2026: a defined constellation, a regulatory pathway and a first-order business case.

Constellation

Seven near-polar planes of sixteen satellites at 700 km. It is the cheapest design we found that covers every point on Earth every minute and delivers 99% of alarms within 100 ms. We picked it with an NSGA-II search over 3,064 designs (cost, latency, coverage, RF vs laser crosslinks) on MeluXina, then re-simulated it for 24 h over 2,000 points.

Over open ocean no gateway is in reach, so alarms hop over the crosslinks to a satellite above an authorised gateway. RF beats laser at every laser price we tested: a laser design with the same service costs €125M more.

Payload and software

Full regenerative NB-IoT gNB on every satellite (16U, 30 kg, 100 W). Each satellite runs KubeEdge EdgeCore, so policies, gNB software and AI models ship as containers and keep running through link outages.

The Consultant's Dilemma

A local gateway may be the best technical choice but still be off-limits because of national restrictions, data-residency rules or licensing. Lynq applies routing policy onboard and sends each flow only to a policy-compliant gateway. Under MNO partnerships, the MNO keeps lawful intercept.

ITU Radio Regulations Article 9 coordination and Article 22 EPFD limits are hard design constraints. A deorbit burn is budgeted, because 700 km decays too slowly. For NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act, updates are signed container images over mTLS, with audited rollouts. Sovereignty and security are built into the software.

Two layers of customers

OQ Technology buys the constellation design, NTN-Pulse, KubeEdge orchestration and support. OQ's end customers come first from offshore energy and maritime, then from defence and sovereign users.

Revenue

A platform licence per satellite or gateway node per year, an annual support contract, and a per-device fee for AI-enabled terminals. End customers pay OQ per asset, never per byte.

We asked for €450k to reach TRL 5 in 12 months: a KubeEdge testbed and hardware-in-the-loop at OQ's 5G Satellite Test Lab.
